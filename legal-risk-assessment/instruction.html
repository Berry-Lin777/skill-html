Matter intake

Start with facts, context, and thresholds.

The score is the end of the intake—not the beginning. Provide enough matter-specific context to explain both dimensions and identify the conditions that could change them.

Scenario

What may happen?

The issue, known facts, relevant history, current status, and business objective.

Exposure

What could be affected?

Financial value, operations, reputation, regulated activity, people, data, and leadership.

Probability signals

Why might it materialize?

Precedent, trigger events, counterparty behavior, deadlines, inquiries, and present conditions.

Governance context

Which rules shape the decision?

Jurisdiction, internal risk tolerance, industry context, existing controls, owners, and review deadlines.

Recommended prompt

Use Legal Risk Assessment to triage this vendor contract issue. State the assumed jurisdiction and any missing facts. Rate severity and likelihood separately with rationale, calculate the initial score, identify contributing and mitigating factors, propose mitigation options, estimate residual risk, assign an owner and monitoring triggers, and identify whether senior or outside counsel review is required.

Assessment workflow

Score, act, then reassess.

A useful assessment preserves the reasoning behind the rating and continues through mitigation, ownership, residual exposure, and escalation.

01

Define the risk

Write a clear risk description, separate known facts from assumptions, and record the business and legal context.

02

Rate severity

Score impact from 1 to 5 using financial, operational, reputational, regulatory, and personal-liability considerations.

03

Rate likelihood

Score probability from 1 to 5 using precedent, foreseeable triggers, current conditions, and the strength of any pattern.

04

Classify the score

Multiply severity by likelihood, map the result to Low, Medium, High, or Critical, and retain the rationale for each input.

05

Design mitigation

Compare controls or negotiation options by effectiveness and effort, then identify the recommended approach.

06

Own and monitor

Assign a named owner, deadline, review cadence, trigger events, contingency plan, and expected residual risk.

07

Escalate proportionately

Brief senior counsel, leadership, the Board, insurers, regulators, or outside counsel when the facts or policy require it.

Deliverables

A record that supports action.

The skill can produce a formal assessment memo and a concise risk-register entry, preserving what was decided, why, by whom, and what happens next.

Assessment

Reasoned classification

Risk description, facts, severity and likelihood ratings, score, contributing factors, and mitigating factors.

Response

Mitigation options

Alternatives compared by effectiveness and effort, a recommended approach, and expected residual risk.

Accountability

Monitoring plan

Named owners, deadlines, review cadence, status, next review date, and events that trigger reassessment.

Governance

Escalation record

Who needs briefing, whether outside counsel is indicated, and any contingency, preservation, insurance, or reporting step.

Outside counsel

Escalate by trigger, not intuition alone.

The framework distinguishes mandatory engagement from matters where specialist review is strongly recommended or should be considered.

Mandatory engagement

Active litigation, government investigations, criminal exposure, securities issues, and Board-level matters. These require qualified counsel and appropriate governance handling.

Strongly recommended

Novel law, unfamiliar or conflicting jurisdictions, material exposure above tolerance, specialist fields, major regulatory changes, and significant M&A.

Consider engagement

Material contract disputes, employment claims, possible data-breach notification, IP disputes, and insurance coverage disagreements.

Appropriate use

A triage aid, not a legal conclusion.

Use the skill to make legal workflow decisions consistent and visible. Do not use its score as a substitute for jurisdiction-specific analysis or professional judgment.

Well suited to

  • Contract deviations, deal exposure, and negotiation issues
  • Regulatory, data privacy, IP, employment, or litigation triage
  • Risk-register entries and recurring portfolio reviews
  • Choosing owners, mitigation actions, cadence, and escalation routes
  • Preparing a structured matter summary for qualified counsel

Do not rely on it for

  • Legal advice, definitive interpretation, or predictions of outcome
  • Emergency response without immediate qualified counsel involvement
  • Scoring without adequate facts, jurisdiction, or company risk thresholds
  • Deciding that privilege exists merely because a memo is labeled privileged
  • Replacing required Board, regulator, insurer, or specialist review

Privilege & confidentiality

Handle sensitive matters deliberately.

The memo format includes a privilege field, but treatment depends on the governing law, purpose, participants, and actual handling of the communication.

Marking is not the same as creating privilege.

Confirm privilege strategy with qualified counsel. Record the assessor, date, purpose, intended audience, and applicable handling instructions; distribute only through approved channels to authorized recipients.

Do not add unnecessary personal, confidential, or regulated information to the assessment. Follow organizational retention, litigation-hold, access-control, and incident-response requirements.

Jurisdiction materially affects both the underlying legal analysis and privilege rules. State the assumed jurisdiction and escalate uncertainty rather than silently generalizing.

A score starts the decision. Ownership, mitigation, monitoring, and escalation complete it.
