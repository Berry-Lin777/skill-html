Question banks are everywhere. Candidates still freeze when asked to “tell me more.”

Interview guides, question banks, STAR templates, and general-purpose models already exist. Real interviews are harder because the follow-ups keep drilling into your own resume.

A polished resume is not a spoken answer

A two-line project can become a dozen follow-ups: what you owned, why you chose that approach, where the metric came from, and whether the result can be verified.

Scripted answers break under pressure

Candidates memorize nice answers, then struggle when the interviewer rephrases the question, asks for details, or follows the thread one layer deeper.

Human mock interviews are not always available

Friends may not know the role. Coaches require scheduling and cost more. The market needs a middle layer between solo question drills and live mock interviews.

Stop drilling random questions. Put the resume under pressure.

When question generation becomes basic infrastructure, the real edge comes from personalization, continuity, and factual constraints.

Traditional prep: many questions, weak personal fit
