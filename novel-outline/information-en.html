THE INSPECTION · 14 GATES

Fourteen gates, all enforced in code

A checklist left to model self-discipline is unreliable. Every gate is a deterministic check inside validate, not prose that the model may interpret or ignore.

GateDefault threshold or requirement
Lead characters1–5, including protagonists and primary antagonist
Named supporting charactersMaximum 10
Functional charactersMaximum 10; face-bearing roles identified by labels rather than unnecessary names
Primary settings4 + ⌈episodes/10⌉, constrained between 5 and 15
Narrative propsMaximum 8 by default; the gate is explicitly skipped when props are omitted
One-use settingsMust include a practical reuse or redressing plan
Beat spacingNo story-beat gap greater than 3 episodes by default
Episode-one hookRequired
Major-turn timingThe earliest major beat cannot be delayed until the final episode
Three episode fieldsSynopsis, hook, and suspense are all required
Multi-character scenesThree or more tracked characters require a staging plan
Generation risksRain, crowds, physical contact, hand close-ups, and similar risks must be flagged
Reference integrityNo unemployed character, unused setting, zero-use prop, or missing ID
Outline formNarrative prose without quoted dialogue; screenplay writing belongs downstream

Platform-specific limits can override individual values through params.thresholds without modifying the validator. Every gate has an intentional failure fixture in the self-test suite.

Why character tiers matter

A single total confuses audience memory with visual-asset cost. The lead cap controls how many central figures viewers must retain. Supporting and functional caps control the number of consistent character faces the AI production pipeline must maintain.

Why AI short drama allows more settings

AI-generated settings do not follow the economics of physical set construction. The primary-setting cap protects cross-episode consistency and spatial clarity while still allowing the visual variety that generated environments make possible.

THE CHECKPOINT · USER DECISION

The most valuable step: approve before expanding

The quick skeleton presents exactly three decisions: what was cut, who was merged, and which episodes contain the major turning points. Before any full episode writing, it must pass validate --stage beats.

DRAMATURGY REVIEW

Why the workflow pauses

A wrong skeleton costs one planning pass. Discovering the same problem after dozens of episodes have been drafted forces character, hook, setting, and asset decisions to be rebuilt together. Episode drafting is also batched in groups of no more than ten to reduce late-run drift.

THE PRODUCTION DOSSIER · REPORT

One report connects creative review with production planning

The report is a wide, searchable review surface. It is not merely a screenshot: it exposes the structural data used for decisions and diagnosis.

KPI Band

Total episodes, story beats, character tiers, primary settings, generation risks, and adaptation mode.

Beat Rhythm

Major beats use solid markers; regular beats use lighter marks; pacing gaps are drawn directly on the axis.

Episode Overview

Expandable cards contain synopsis, hook, suspense, setting, character, beat, and warning tags.

Setting Overview

Each location shows episode appearances, story-beat load, characters, and one-use reuse plans.

Key Decisions

Approved cuts, merges, lead roster, and major-turn positions are generated from the underlying structure.

Scheduling Matrix

Characters and settings share one grid. Reading a single episode column reveals the production requirement.

Asset Conversion

Character tiers, environments, props, and generation difficulties become a production workload estimate.

Quality & Export

Header badge, failure banner, complete gate list, and a JSON export containing the original outline data.

Offline by design: visualizations are embedded as SVG and CSS. The report opens locally without external chart libraries, APIs, or an internet connection.

THE EVIDENCE · TRACEABILITY

Every major adaptation decision can be traced back to the novel

Important retained elements use keep[].evidence. The evidence is verbatim, allowing editors to verify the adaptation against the supplied text rather than trust a vague memory of the book.

What · Retained element

Darcy’s first proposal and Elizabeth’s rejection

Why · Dramatic function

The scene concentrates desire, pride, class prejudice, Jane’s heartbreak, and Wickham’s accusation into the central collision.

Evidence · Source text

In vain have I struggled. It will not do. My feelings will not be repressed. You must allow me to tell you how ardently I admire and love you.

No adaptation built from a title alone. No missing plot filled in from memory.

THE DIAGNOSIS · EXISTING OUTLINES

Already have an outline? Diagnose it without regenerating it.

Convert the existing plan to outline.json and run checkup. The quality panel becomes a structural diagnostic report. Failed gates do not prevent HTML rendering because exposing the problems is the purpose of the mode.

node scripts/novel-outline.mjs checkup outline.json node scripts/novel-outline.mjs render outline.json --html --lang en > outline-report.html

Built for long manuscripts

When a novel cannot fit in one context window, chunk splits it by chapter, normally fifteen chapters per volume. Each volume is summarized and then consolidated. If chapter headings cannot be detected, the script falls back to size-based splitting. It supports up to sixty volumes and reports truncation explicitly.
