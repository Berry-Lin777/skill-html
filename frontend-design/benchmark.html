Source evidence

Six requirements are written into the design contract

These are verified source facts: the instruction explicitly requires them. They show what the skill asks an agent to do, but not whether a future interface actually satisfies the request.

Contract 01

Subject grounding

Name a concrete subject, its audience and the page’s single job before choosing a direction.

Contract 02

Distinctive direction

Derive palette, typography and structure from the subject rather than a reusable visual default.

Contract 03

Design planning

Define 4–6 named colors, at least two type roles, layout concepts and one memorable signature.

Contract 04

Self-critique

Challenge generic choices before building, then critique the implementation again after the first pass.

Contract 05

Accessible baseline

Support mobile widths, visible keyboard focus and reduced-motion preferences as a quality floor.

Contract 06

Interface writing

Use active, consistent action language and make errors and empty states tell people what to do next.

Release decision

Three gates keep taste connected to evidence

A high visual score cannot rescue a broken flow, and technical completeness cannot substitute for a subject-specific point of view. Release requires all three gates.

1

Direction gate

The design plan names its subject logic, tests itself against defaults and commits to one justified signature.

2

Experience gate

Primary and edge-state journeys are understandable, responsive and operable with keyboard and reduced motion.

3

Evidence gate

Screenshots, viewport checks and reviewer notes support the final claims; untested areas remain labeled.
