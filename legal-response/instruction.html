Supported inquiries

One workflow, distinct legal contexts.

Choose an inquiry type when invoking the skill. If the category is ambiguous or omitted, the skill should show the available options and ask before choosing a response path.

DSR

Data subject requests

Access, deletion, correction, portability, opt-out, acknowledgment, denial, or extension.

Hold

Discovery holds

Initial notices, reminders, scope modifications, and releases.

Vendor

Vendor questions

Contract status, amendments, certifications, audits, and insurance certificates.

NDA

NDA requests

Standard forms, counterparty markup, declines, renewals, and extensions.

Privacy

Privacy inquiries

Cookies, notices, sharing, children’s data, and cross-border transfers.

Legal process

Subpoenas

Acknowledgments, objections, extensions, and cover letters—always with counsel review.

Insurance

Claim notifications

Initial notice, supplemental information, and reservation-of-rights responses.

Custom

Required details

A template never excuses missing facts.

Every response must use the correct names, dates, reference numbers, facts, law, jurisdiction, deadline, signature block, and recipient-specific next steps.

Identity & matter

Who, what, and which record?

Recipient, requester or custodian, contact details, matter or request ID, agreement, policy, case, or claim reference.

Law & timing

Where, under what rule, by when?

Applicable jurisdiction and regulation, date received, legally verified deadline, urgency, and any extension basis.

Substance & audience

What must this reply actually say?

Specific facts, requested action, scope, relationship, sensitivity, desired next step, approver, signer, and contact channel.

Controlled drafting workflow

Check permission before prose.

Escalation happens before customization. The skill does not finish a polished letter and only then discover that the template should never have been used.

01

Identify the inquiry

Resolve the category and sub-category. Ask rather than guess when the request could follow more than one legal path.

02

Load the template

Check local settings such as legal.local.md or the team’s templates directory, then identify every required variable.

03

Run escalation checks

Apply universal triggers and the chosen category’s specific triggers before generating an ordinary response.

04

Gather missing facts

Collect names, dates, references, scope, jurisdiction, law, deadlines, signer, and category-specific details.

05

Verify law and deadline

Confirm that cited regulations, rights, terminology, and response timelines match the applicable jurisdiction and receipt date.

06

Customize the draft

Populate every variable, reflect the actual facts, adjust tone to audience and sensitivity, and state clear recipient next steps.

07

Present for review

Return the draft, escalation result, and follow-up list. Regulated communications and all subpoena responses require qualified counsel review.

When a template exists

Use the approved structure and metadata.

Load its use case, variables, body, escalation triggers, follow-up actions, version, and last-reviewed date—then customize every required field.

When no template exists

Say so; do not invent team approval.

Provide a reasonable category-specific structure, offer to create a template, and route the new template through legal-team review before future reuse.

Escalation safeguards

Know when the template stops.

These are decision triggers, not optional warnings. Detection changes the output from a routine response into a clearly labeled counsel-review draft and escalation recommendation.

Universal triggers

  • Potential litigation, regulatory investigation, or criminal liability
  • Regulator, government agency, or law-enforcement sender
  • Possible binding commitment, waiver, or executive / Board involvement
  • Media attention, unprecedented handling, or conflicting jurisdictions

DSR and privacy triggers

  • A minor, regulator, active employee dispute, or litigation-held data
  • Unusually broad scope, unique jurisdiction, or special-category data
  • Children’s data, biometric, genetic, health, or complex transfer issues

Hold, vendor, and NDA triggers

  • Unclear or disputed preservation scope, related prior hold, or operational conflict
  • Dispute, threatened litigation, breach, waiver, regulation, or live negotiation impact
  • Competitor, classified information, M&A, AI training data, or biometric subject matter

Subpoena / legal process

  • Always requires individualized counsel review
  • Privilege, third-party data, cross-border production, or unreasonable timeline
  • Templates are starting frameworks, never final legal-process responses
Stop

Do not generate an ordinary templated reply.

Alert

Tell the user that a trigger was detected.

Explain

Name the trigger and why it matters.

Recommend

Identify the appropriate counsel or team path.

Offer

Provide only a “DRAFT — FOR COUNSEL REVIEW” if useful.

Deliverables

A draft plus its control record.

The response itself is only one part of the output. The user also receives the escalation result and operational steps required after review or sending.

Generated response

  • Audience-appropriate tone, caveats, contact, and next step

Control record

What happens next

  • Required reviewer or approver

  • Tracking, logging, and follow-up actions
Every generated response is shown to the user for review before sending; regulated communications require qualified legal review.

Appropriate use

For repeatable inquiries—not automatic legal judgment.

The skill is valuable when the legal team has a known response category, a review path, and enough facts to customize the draft safely.

Well suited to

  • Routine inquiries covered by a current, approved team template
  • Gathering required variables and normalizing a response structure
  • Checking deadlines, escalation conditions, and follow-up steps
  • Creating a reviewable first draft for legal-team refinement
  • Building a new template specification for approval and future use

Do not treat it as

  • Legal advice or a substitute for qualified, jurisdiction-specific analysis
  • Authority to send a response, make a commitment, waive rights, or disclose data
  • A final subpoena, litigation, regulatory, criminal, or Board-level response
  • Proof that a deadline or legal citation is correct without verification
  • A reason to place confidential or privileged information in unapproved tools

Dependencies & limits

The template and the law both change.

Safe use depends on current team-approved templates, accurate matter details, verified jurisdictional rules, authorized systems, and human review.

Operational boundaries

Template currency

Templates are living documents. Track version, approver, and last-reviewed date; update or retire them when law, policy, or practice changes.

Jurisdiction

Rights, terminology, deadlines, objections, and required notices must be verified for the actual jurisdiction and facts.

Confidentiality

Use approved channels and access controls. A privilege label alone does not establish privilege or authorize disclosure.

Final authority

The skill drafts and flags; qualified legal professionals decide, approve, and send regulated or consequential communications.

Connectors are optional and tool-agnostic. Email can be used to create a draft, calendar to schedule deadline reminders, and approved chat, storage, CLM, CRM, e-signature, office, or project systems to support the team workflow. The skill should offer these actions only when the relevant connector is actually available; it must not assume a named product or send automatically.

A strong legal response says the right thing—and knows when not to say it yet.
