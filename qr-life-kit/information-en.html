QR Life Kit · Information

Turn everyday details into QR codes people can actually use

QR Life Kit creates static QR codes and matching cards for websites, Wi-Fi, contacts, email, SMS, phone numbers, locations, calendar events, and plain text—all on your own machine.

The result is more than one QR image: you get files for sharing, files for print, and a scan report for the finished artwork.

The payload is stored directly in the QR. There is no short-link service in the middle. Contact, Wi-Fi, and event details are not encrypted, so share them with the same care as the original information.

Why not use a random QR website?

Making a quick URL code is easy. The harder questions come later: Will it stay sharp in print? Does it still scan after you place it on a card? Is the link tied to someone else's redirect service? Did you upload a Wi-Fi password or contact record to a third party?

QR Life Kit keeps the whole job together and local: prepare the payload, build the QR, lay out the card, scan the finished artwork, and save everything in one output folder.

One workflow covers screen sharing, printed material, and event signage, even when the payload changes.

1. Describe what you need

You do not need to learn vCard syntax or Wi-Fi escaping rules. Tell your Agent what the QR is for, which details it should contain, and where it will be used. The Agent chooses the payload type and asks only for the fields that matter.

2. Build the QR and the card

Each run creates a standalone QR plus a card for sharing or print. Built-in options include paper, midnight, and sunset themes, with square or portrait formats.

The code itself stays dark on white with a proper quiet zone. Logos, gradients, and decoration belong around the QR—not over it.

3. Scan the finished artwork

A generated QR is only the first checkpoint. QR Life Kit decodes both qr.png and the final card.png, then compares each result with the original payload. verification.json says passed only when both match.

Verification reads the payload. It does not open a website, join Wi-Fi, send a message, place a call, or add a contact.

Who is it for?

Shops and event teams can make menu, Wi-Fi, and calendar cards. Freelancers and conference attendees can make contact cards. Teams can turn links, locations, or phone numbers into consistent printed material.

If you only need a quick QR, the build is short. If the file is going to a client or a printer, the complete output and final-artwork scan check are where the kit earns its keep.
