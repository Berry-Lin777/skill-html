Pretty fake shots do not sell real products

The biggest risk in software product films is looking impressive while no one understands what the product actually does. The interface has to be real, the flow has to be legible, and the shots must remain editable; otherwise even beautiful AI visuals only sidestep the product itself.

AI visuals look good,
but the product is fake

Viewers want to see real pages, real features, and real outcomes. A fictional UI, however polished, has a hard time building trust.

Editing tools are mature,
but the labor is heavy

Scripts, storyboards, screenshots, camera moves, captions, sound effects, beat timing, and revisions all need attention. Small teams rarely have time to make a polished film for every release.

Remotion is powerful,
but shot language is hard

Programmatic rendering solves how to generate the film, but not how to make it feel like an ad. Shots, rhythm, and sound still need a mature method.

Product video needs to move from generation to delivery

What teams need is not a lucky one-off clip, but a finished project that can be revised, reused, and tracked in version control.

Workflow

The user provides a product directory, website, screenshots, recordings, audio, or copy. The Skill turns those inputs into a storyboard, project, finished film, and an adjustable workbench.

STEP 01

Install the Skill

Load the shot cards, templates, components, audio, and production notes.

STEP 02

Prepare the assets

Provide the project directory, website URL, screenshots, recordings, product positioning, and must-show features.

STEP 03

Clarify the brief

Define the theme, structure, style, duration, aspect ratio, language, BGM, and output requirements.

STEP 04

Choose the mode

Pick the Ink Press template, autonomous creation, or guided collaboration based on project risk.

STEP 05

Stage the pages

Freeze demo data, capture real page states, and avoid blurred text, overlaps, and sensitive information.

STEP 06

Render and deliver

Deliver the project directory, BGM and no-BGM films, QA notes, and Motion Workbench.
